## Findings (hand-written; kept when the tables above are regenerated)

**The full probabilistic snowfall suite is on the 01/07/13/19Z cycles** (in `core`; there
is no separate product directory). Those cycles carry, to f257–f263 (~day 11):

- ASNOW 6-h percentiles (1…99) and exceedances (>0.1…>12 in), every 6 h of valid time;
- ASNOW 24-h, 48-h and 72-h percentiles (1, 5, 10, 20, 25, 30, 40, 50, 60, 70, 75, 80, 90,
  95, 99) and exceedance probabilities — 24 h: >0.1, 1, 2, 4, 6, 8, 10, 12, 18, 24 in;
  48 h: >1, 2, 4, 6, 8, 12, 18, 24, 30, 36 in; 72 h: >1, 4, 6, 8, 12, 18, 24, 30, 36, 48 in —
  windows ending every 6 h to ~f083, then every 12 h (valid 00Z/12Z);
- SNOWLVL and SNOWLR percentiles to f263.

The 00/06/12/18Z cycles have snowfall percentiles only to f048/f054 (6-h windows) and no
24/48/72-h snowfall windows, but they are the only cycles with `qmd` (QPF percentiles,
0…100 by 5, in 6/12/24-h and 1–3-day blocks). The odd-hour cycles in between (02–05Z …)
have neither. Files: odd cycles are hourly to f264; synoptic cycles hourly to f048, then
3-hourly to f192 and 6-hourly to f264. Later files repeat some earlier windows (e.g. f251
holds the 24-h window ending f227 again) with slightly different values; we only read a
window from the file of its own end hour.

The qmd day blocks differ by cycle: 12Z f072 has 0–3, 1–3 and 2–3 day blocks, 18Z f072
only 2–3 day — the cause of the CI failure (`qmd f072: no APCP surface Noneh pct 10`).

## Validation against the GSL NBM 1-D viewer (01Z 8 Nov 2026)

`tools/nbm_validate_gsl.py` decodes NBM at the nearest 2.5-km cell and compares with the
viewer's station CSVs (apps.gsl.noaa.gov/nbmviewer/data/archive/2026/11/08/NBM5.0/01/<ID>.csv)
for 24/48/72-h windows, 6 valid times each, percentiles 10/50/90 and P(>1, >4, >8 in).
Station coordinates from IEM (rounded to 0.01°).

| Station | Percentiles (24/48/72 h × p10/p50/p90 × 6 times) | Exceedance P(>1/4/8 in) |
|---|---|---|
| KMLP Mullan Pass ID | **54/54 exactly equal** | 15/54 equal, max diff 12 points |
| KSMP Stampede Pass WA | **54/54 exactly equal** | 40/54 equal, max diff 8 points |
| KTRK Truckee CA | 30/54 equal, max diff 0.98 in | 4/54 equal, max diff 53 points |

- **Percentiles: reproduced exactly** where our station location falls in the same cell as
  the viewer's (KMLP, KSMP). KTRK sits on a cell edge (fraction 0.97) and none of the four
  surrounding cells matches the viewer's numbers, so the viewer uses a slightly different
  station location (or point processing) there.
- **Exceedance probabilities: not reproduced.** The viewer's values match neither NBM's
  probability message for the window (10 %), nor its later duplicate (13 %), nor a value
  derived from the viewer's own percentiles (e.g. KTRK P(>1 in) 39 % with p90 = 1.46 in).
  The probability fields are clearly separate NBM products (consistent with NBM calibrating
  them independently); which copy/processing the viewer shows is unresolved.

## What this means for the extended-range method

The derived "likely range" (QPF 25–75 × SLR × P(snow)) was only needed because we read the
00/06/12/18Z cycles. Reading 01/07/13/19Z instead gives NBM's own snowfall percentiles for
every 12-h period to ~day 11 and real 24/72-h windows for the totals. On the 01Z 8 Nov run the
deterministic 12-h amount fell inside NBM's 10–90 % snowfall range in 100 % of 31 wet periods
(≤ 48 h) and 99.8 % of 4,927 periods beyond (`data/points/calibration.json`), versus 31 % inside
the derived likely range on 12Z 7 Nov. Trade-off: those cycles have no QPF percentiles, so
liquid-equivalent ranges are only available from a synoptic cycle.
